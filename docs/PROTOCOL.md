# Research protocol, fixed before experiment execution

Question: under a structural intervention that suppresses responses without changing the surviving response mechanisms, can randomized retention of a completion prefix identify the effects of all deletions of at most r endpoints, and how should retention be allocated?

Primary estimand: finite evaluation-cohort mean change in binary correctness, relative to the first response, for every nonempty deletion set of size <=r. Independent per-request audit randomization is the inferential source; dataset generalization is not claimed. Primary loss: maximum conditional audit variance across the prespecified deletion menu. Outcomes, ordering and potential responses are fixed during audit repetition.

Theory: universal deterministic depth r+1 is necessary/sufficient; inverse inclusion estimator; exact audit variance; exchangeable-order sharp worst-case envelope; cost-constrained monotone allocation with independently checkable primal/dual certificates. No claim of optimality over all estimators or history-dependent designs.

Primary empirical study: sklearn digits, breast_cancer, wine. Stratified train/pilot/evaluation split fractions 0.50/0.20/0.30; seeds 100..109. Six frozen models: GaussianNB, logistic regression, random forest, extra trees, k-nearest neighbours, SVC. Real trained predictions; engineered latency environments (exchangeable exponential, heterogeneous lognormal, correctness-correlated lognormal), not measured provider latency. r=3; 41 deletion targets; completion budgets .25, .5, 1, 2 additional observed responses per request. Pilot-only design. Comparators: equal-probability full-prefix retention (up to r+1); geometric rank probabilities; exchangeable minimax; pilot minimax; held-out oracle minimax diagnostic. Floors q>=0.005. Oracle is never an operational baseline.

Secondary: synthetic exact formula grid m=8,16,32,64; r=1,2,4 subject r<m, b=.1,.25,.5,1 subject b<r. All ranks uniform in theorem calculations. Optimization verification: random matrices vs independently optimized primal; enumerate permutation/deletion/outcome witnesses for m<=6; exact rational HT expectation/variance checks. Monte Carlo: 500 audit repetitions for three seed-100 environments/datasets at budget .5. Same audit uniforms across methods. Report bias, RMSE, 95% normal-interval coverage as a diagnostic, without claiming exact finite-sample coverage.

Software execution: an asynchronous six-endpoint harness returns rank-one response while auditing a sampled prefix and cancelling remaining futures; exercise all R=1..4, multiple masks, and replay equality. Simulated service delays are explicitly labelled. A load-coupling counterexample shows invariance failure; it is a boundary, not something weighting solves.

Report all prespecified conditions including negative transfer of exchangeable or pilot designs. No paid APIs, model training on test labels, human studies, or fabricated production claims. Search novelty against competing risks, algorithm portfolio censoring, inverse-probability sampling, randomized truncation, multivariate sampling allocation, and causal policy comparison. Public manuscript claims are conditional on the stated SCM, not absolute discovery or patentability.

## Theory-driven extension after primary results
The primary evaluation finished without a universal empirical advantage: pilot allocations can fail on sparse calibration data and normal intervals undercover. Retain these results. Add an exact structural comparison of identical winner-only logs under exchangeable versus paired orders (r=2), plus a sharp worst-case allocation given only winner identities. These are analytic results, independently checked by permutation enumeration, not post-selected empirical wins. Record them separately in results/twins.json. Do not describe normal intervals as valid confidence guarantees. Main inference guarantees use exact audit variance and conservative Hoeffding bounds.
